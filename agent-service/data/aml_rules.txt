AML & CFT COMPLIANCE RULES — LOANFLOW REFERENCE DOCUMENT
Source: PMLA 2002, RBI Master Direction on KYC, FATF Recommendations
Last updated: 2024

=== SECTION 1: ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING FRAMEWORK ===

1.1 Regulatory Basis
LoanFlow operates under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) 2002 and the rules thereunder. All employees involved in customer onboarding, credit appraisal, and disbursement must be trained in AML/CFT compliance annually.

1.2 Suspicious Transaction Reporting
Any transaction that gives rise to reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorist financing must be reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit — India (FIU-IND) within 7 days of detection. Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) must be filed regardless of the amount involved.

1.3 Customer Risk Categorisation
All customers must be classified into risk categories based on:
- Country of origin / residence
- Nature of business or profession
- Transaction patterns
- Source of funds
Low risk: Salaried individuals, government employees, established businesses.
Medium risk: Self-employed, high-value transactions, frequent transfers.
High risk: PEPs, NRIs, cash-intensive businesses, customers from high-risk jurisdictions.

=== SECTION 2: POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS (PEP) ===

2.1 PEP Definition
A PEP is an individual who is or has been entrusted with prominent public functions in India or abroad. This includes: heads of state, senior politicians, senior government officials, judicial officials, military officials, senior executives of state-owned corporations, and important political party officials.

2.2 PEP Screening Requirement
All loan applicants must be screened against PEP lists before onboarding. PEP screening must also cover immediate family members (spouse, children, parents) and close associates.

2.3 Enhanced Due Diligence for PEPs
PEP customers automatically qualify as high risk. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) is mandatory:
- Senior management approval required before onboarding
- Source of wealth must be verified
- Ongoing monitoring at monthly intervals
- All transactions above ₹10 lakh require documented rationale

2.4 Foreign PEPs
Foreign PEPs (individuals who hold or have held prominent positions in foreign countries) require the same treatment as domestic PEPs. EDD must include verification of the individual's role and a review of the country's risk rating.

=== SECTION 3: SANCTIONS SCREENING ===

3.1 UNSC Sanctions List
All applicants must be screened against the United Nations Security Council (UNSC) consolidated sanctions list. No business relationship may be established with sanctioned individuals, entities, or countries.

3.2 Indian Sanctions
Applicants must be screened against sanctions designated by the Ministry of Home Affairs under the Unlawful Activities Prevention Act (UAPA) and lists maintained by FIU-IND.

3.3 OFAC and Other International Lists
For loans above ₹25 lakh, applicants must also be screened against OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control), EU consolidated list, and UK HM Treasury sanctions list.

3.4 Screening Frequency
- On application: Full screening against all applicable lists
- Ongoing: Monthly automated screening for active loan accounts
- On material change: Re-screen whenever customer details change (address, employment, etc.)

=== SECTION 4: SOURCE OF FUNDS VERIFICATION ===

4.1 Documentation Requirements
For loans above ₹5 lakh, the source of the applicant's income and assets must be documented and verified. Acceptable documents include: salary slips, ITR, Form 16, bank statements, CA certificate for self-employed.

4.2 Cash Income
Cash-based income is considered high risk. Self-employed borrowers reporting primarily cash income must provide additional documentation: GST returns, trade references, property ownership documents.

4.3 Unusual Income Patterns
Bank statements showing large, irregular, round-number deposits inconsistent with stated income must trigger enhanced review. Salary credits that don't match stated employer must be investigated.

4.4 Related Party Transactions
Loans where the borrower and guarantor are related parties require special scrutiny. Loans to employees or shareholders of the lending institution require board approval.

=== SECTION 5: TRANSACTION MONITORING ===

5.1 Monitoring Thresholds
Cash Transaction Reports (CTRs) must be filed for all cash transactions above ₹10 lakh per day. Cross-border Wire Transfer Reports (WTRs) must be filed for international transfers above USD 25,000 or equivalent.

5.2 Structuring Detection
Transactions structured to avoid reporting thresholds (smurfing) must be flagged. Any series of transactions by the same customer that appear designed to circumvent the ₹10 lakh threshold must trigger an STR.

5.3 Velocity Checks
Multiple loan applications within a 30-day window by the same individual or entity must be reviewed. Unusual repayment patterns (prepaying loans immediately after disbursement) must be reported.

5.4 Geographic Risk
Transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions (FATF grey-listed or blacklisted countries) must receive enhanced scrutiny. All such transactions require senior compliance officer review.

=== SECTION 6: RECORD KEEPING ===

6.1 Retention Period
All KYC documents, transaction records, and STRs must be retained for a minimum of 5 years after the end of the customer relationship or after the date of the transaction, whichever is later.

6.2 Accessibility
Records must be maintained in a manner that allows retrieval within 48 hours for regulatory inspection. Electronic records are acceptable but must be backed up and protected from tampering.

6.3 Audit Trail
A complete audit trail of all AML checks, their outcomes, and any escalations must be maintained. The audit trail must capture the date, time, user ID, and outcome of each check.
